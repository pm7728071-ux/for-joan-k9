Proud of you

Things I have watched you survive

Not one of these was easy. You did them anyway.

  1. Chapter One

    You left everything you knew

    You got on a plane to a country where nobody knew your name, where the language on the street was not yours and the food did not taste like home. You did not do it because you were fearless. You did it because you were brave, and there is a difference. Fearless people feel nothing. Brave people feel all of it and go anyway.

  2. Chapter Two

    You do work nobody applauds

    You keep another family's home running: the cleaning, the cooking, the order, the thousand small things that only get noticed when they are missing. That work is real work. It takes patience, brains and heart, and you do it far from the people who would have thanked you for it every single day.

  3. Chapter Three

    You cry, and you still show up

    There are mornings you wake up heavy and still put on the uniform and go. That is the hardest kind of strength there is, the kind nobody claps for. On the days you feel like you are failing, hear me: you are not failing. You are carrying something heavy, and you are still walking.

  4. Chapter Four

    You are coming home different

    Not broken. Not smaller. Different in the way iron is different after the fire. You will come back knowing you can survive things you once thought you could not, and nobody will ever be able to take that from you. I cannot wait to meet that version of you.

Joan, read this slowly

If you are reading this on a hard day, I want you to stop for a second and look at where you are. You are in a country that is not yours, doing a job that takes more out of you than anyone there understands, and you are still going. Not everyone could do that. Not everyone would.

I know some days the distance wins. I know there are nights you cry quietly so nobody hears, and mornings you want to pack everything and come home. That does not make you weak. It makes you human. Feel it, then get up, because you have a whole family here whose day gets better just knowing you are still out there fighting.

You are not a burden. You are not a disappointment. You are not behind. You are my sister, my only sister, and I am proud of you in a way I do not have enough words for. Keep going. March is coming, and I will be there.

Your only brother, Peter.
